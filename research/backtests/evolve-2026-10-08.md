# Strategy review (Thu Oct 8, 2026)

Vamsi, Oct 8: evolve the strategies, learn from the mistakes, come up with new ones. This is what was tested, what
changed and what did not. Every number below is a replay or backtest on past sessions, not a forecast.

## What went wrong

* The paper book's five closed trades this week were all stopped (POET, UMAC, CIFR, HUT, APLD), and every
  position was a high-beta name from the same corner of the market (crypto miners, AI infrastructure, speculative
  tech). On Oct 8 that group fell together: 25 of the 67 radar and candidate names moved 1+ ATR down, bitcoin -3%,
  while the S&P 500 lost about 0.7%. Supports do not hold when the whole group is sold.
* The stops sat about 0.6 ATR under the entries, inside a normal day's range: the limit-order test (RUNBOOK 2c) had
  already found such stops hit within 3 sessions 62% of the time.
* Deeper: the radar's entries have no measurable edge of their own. The 5-year level backtest gave +0.02R a trade
  against +0.01R for random entries with the same stop and target; any intraday entry held to the close lost about
  0.3% after costs (dip test). The new lab below shows the candidates' own drift was worth more than the radar's
  timing.

* Where the money is: over 5 years the candidates' whole return came overnight (close to open +0.20% a night,
  open to close -0.02%). A close 1.5+ ATR under the prior close rebounded +0.83% overnight before costs (+0.53%
  after, 58% up; 2+ ATR: +1.10%, 61% up). Weighting each day equally the edge shrinks to +0.13% ± 0.45 (+0.48% ±
  0.82 for 2+ ATR): most of it comes from days when the whole group sold off. A stock that fell 1.5+ ATR on a day
  the S&P 500 rose 0.5%+ kept falling the next day (-1.1%): its own bad news, not a sell-off.
* The night strategy has the smallest worst windows of anything tested, but a small mean, and on 5-minute bars
  over the last 30 sessions it lost 2.8% (Oct 6: four night buys gapped down together on Oct 7).
* No strategy doubled $1,000 in any window. Momentum's best window made +84% and its worst lost 51%.

* Adopted: the real book's night sleeve (RUNBOOK 2e). It adds buys at the 15:55 run in names that fell 1.5+ ATR and
  trade in the lowest quarter of the day's range, sold at the next open: the overnight rebound is the one return
  source in this universe that shows up in every test, and the real book can reuse its sale proceeds the same day.
  In the paper book (a cash account, T+1) the same sleeve improved the hourly windows but lost 0.4 points on the
  5-minute windows, so the paper rules stay as they are.
* Wider stops (sized on the stop, so the dollars at risk stay the same) shrink the worst windows a lot but cut the
  mean, because fewer trades reach their targets: a trade-off, not an improvement under the rule.
* Trend and breadth gates, which looked like the lesson of Oct 8, did not hold up: the radar's recent gains came from
  dips bought during declines, and the gates skip those too.
* Caveats: the candidates are today's popular names (they rose, which flatters buy-and-hold and every long rule),
  the 5-minute windows overlap, and the differences between most variants are smaller than the spread between
  windows.
