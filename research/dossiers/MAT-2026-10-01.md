**The CEO change: does a sale process explain it?**

Facts (8-K, Sep 30, 2026, exhibit 99.1):
- Ynon Kreiz steps down as Chairman and CEO effective Oct 2, "to take a senior leadership position at another public
  company." Yahoo Finance and Reuters (Oct 1) identify it as co-CEO of the merged Paramount Skydance / Warner Bros.
  Discovery.
- Roger Lynch becomes Chairman on Oct 2 and CEO "on or before November 2, 2026." He is CEO of Condé Nast since 2019, a
  Mattel director since 2018 and its independent lead director.
- Diana Ferguson becomes independent lead director.
- The release cites "a comprehensive succession planning process" and "its next phase of profitable growth." That is
  standalone language. Nothing mentions strategic alternatives.

Read:
- The CEO change is explained by Kreiz leaving for Paramount, not by a sale process. The WSJ says no formal process
  exists. Reuters adds that Lynch's transition "could complicate any deal ... as he works on his strategy."
- Why it may still matter: the timing invites opportunistic approaches. Kreiz is gone on Oct 2, Lynch is part-time until
  he leaves Condé Nast by Nov 2, and the stock had just hit a 52-week low.
- Lynch's record is monetizing brands' intellectual property (IP) at Condé Nast, so a licensing-led buyer might get a
  hearing. That is speculation.
- A new chairman usually wants to present his own plan first. And $20-21 is no higher than where the stock traded in
  January and early February 2026 ($20-22.5).

**Authentic Brands: would it want all of Mattel?**
- **Model.** Authentic owns brands and licenses them out. It buys the IP, collects royalties, and leaves manufacturing,
  wholesale and retail to licensees and operating partners.
- **Deal size.** Its largest deal is Reebok, about $2.5B, closed in 2022. That is widely reported; I did not open a
  dated source today. Guess was about $1.4B including debt (Aug 2025). Mattel at $7.5-8.2B including debt would be
  about 3x Reebok.
- **The company itself.**
  - Valued above $20B in General Atlantic's 2023 round.
  - New CEO since May 20, 2026: Matt Maddox, formerly of Wynn Resorts. Founder Jamie Salter, now executive chairman,
    oversees M&A.
  - An IPO targeted for early 2027.
- **Fit.** Mattel is not an IP shell. It has:
  - about 31,000 employees (Robinhood profile);
  - its own factories, American Girl stores, Mattel Studios and the Mattel163 games studio;
  - licenses taken from Disney, DC, TMNT, KPop Demon Hunters and others, which may carry change-of-control terms (not
    checked).
- **What Authentic would really want** is the IP: Barbie, Hot Wheels, Fisher-Price, UNO, American Girl, Polly Pocket,
  Masters of the Universe and Thomas & Friends.
- **So a whole-company bid would likely need partners**, either an operating partner or private-equity co-investors to
  take the factories and distribution. That adds parties and time.
- **Timing.** Buying an $8B manufacturer with 31,000 staff months before an IPO is a big departure from Authentic's
  model. This is the main reason to doubt the interest turns into a firm bid soon.

**Antitrust and financing.**
- **Antitrust: low risk.** I know of no toy maker that Authentic owns, so there is little direct competitive overlap,
  unlike Hasbro in 2017. There would still be a US antitrust (HSR) filing, with a 30-day initial wait, plus foreign
  reviews. None of this matters by Monday.
- **Financing: hard but possible.**
  - At $20-21, the buyer needs $5.7-6.0B for the equity. Mattel's $2.35B of debt (its own total) would likely need
    refinancing if a leveraged owner cost Mattel its investment-grade rating (I did not check the notes' change-of-control
    terms).
  - That is about $7.5-8.2B before fees, or 9.6-10.5x trailing adjusted EBITDA of $784M.
  - At 5-6x EBITDA of debt, that means about $4-4.7B of debt and $3.5B or more of equity, so Authentic would need
    partners.
  - No financing has been reported. Authentic's own debt was not checked.
