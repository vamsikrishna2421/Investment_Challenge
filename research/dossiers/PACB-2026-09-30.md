**Dilution and financing: the main risk.**
- Cash was $236.9M on Jun 30.
  - Burn: about $52M in Q1 (before the $48.1M asset-sale inflow) and $39M in Q2. That is roughly six quarters at $40M.
  - The 10-Q says cash lasts beyond 12 months, but that the company "may require additional capital resources".
- Convertible notes total $641M of principal:
  - 2029 Notes: $200.0M at 1.50%, due Aug 15, 2029, converting at about $4.89 (204.5157 shares per $1,000). Held by SoftBank's SB
    Northstar. While SoftBank holds at least $180M, covenants restrict new debt; the main exception is up to $75M of secured credit facilities.
  - 2030 Notes: $441.0M at 1.375%, due Dec 15, 2030, converting at about $21.50.
  - At $2.37 both are far below their conversion prices, so they are debt, not dilution, unless swapped for stock. PacBio did that
    in Nov 2024, giving 20.45M new shares, $50M of cash and $200M of new notes for $459M of 2028 notes.
  - Jun 30 fair values: the 2029s at $182.9M (91% of par) and the 2030s at $324.4M (74% of par). The debt market is pricing
    real risk. Holders can put the notes back at par on a fundamental change.
- Shelf: an automatic universal shelf (S-3ASR, filed Feb 25, 2026;
  [EDGAR](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1299130/000110465926019745/tm266949d1_s3asr.htm)) lets PacBio sell stock with a
  same-day prospectus supplement. No ATM agreement or 424B prospectus has been found since.
- A 74% run with no company news, covenants that block new debt, and $641M of notes to deal with: that is when companies sell
  stock or swap debt for it.
- Share count +3.4% in a year, to 310.8M.

**Competition.** Illumina now owns PacBio's former short-read IP, and its 5-base chemistry reads methylation too. Oxford Nanopore
sells long-read sequencing and is supported by the same EpiSign release. Today's news itself shows methylation analysis is not a
PacBio-only feature.
