**What the court actually ruled (read in the opinion itself).**
- On Sep 30, Judge Richard G. Andrews (D. Del.) issued a 55-page *Trial Opinion* in *United Therapeutics v. Liquidia Technologies*, C.A. No. 23-975-RGA ([opinion](https://www.ded.uscourts.gov/sites/ded/files/opinions/23-975_6.pdf)).
  - It is a Hatch-Waxman action, tried without a jury on June 23-26, 2025.
- Liquidia had stipulated to both direct and indirect infringement of claims 1 and 14 of U.S. Patent 11,826,327. So the case turned on whether those claims are valid.
  - Liquidia argued the claims were invalid as obvious, because of a prior sale, and because the prior art already disclosed them. The court rejected all three and found that Liquidia infringes claims 1 and 14.
  - The court held that the dependent claims 5, 6, 9 and 17 "are not entitled to patentable weight", so it did not rule on whether Liquidia infringes them. News reports describe them as invalid.
  - The opinion ends: "The parties should submit an agreed upon form of final judgment within one week", that is, about Oct 7.
- This is a ruling on the merits, not a procedural one.
  - The "motion to strike" in the early GuruFocus headline was Liquidia's motion to strike evidence on who owns the patent. The court denied it in the same decision ([Yahoo/Reuters](https://finance.yahoo.com/healthcare/articles/liquidia-slumps-us-court-rules-194118306.html)).
  - No injunction has been entered yet.
- What the patent covers:
  - Claim 1: improving exercise capacity in a PH-ILD patient by inhaling at least 15 mcg of treprostinil per administration, at 6 mcg or more per breath.
  - Claim 14: the same method using a dry powder inhaler containing treprostinil, which is Yutrepia's format.
  - The patent expires Feb 3, 2042 ([UTHR 10-Q](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1082554/000108255426000027/uthr-20260630.htm); [Google Patents](https://patents.google.com/patent/US11826327B2/en)).
- Liquidia's release went out at 1:20 PM ET ([copy](https://www.stocktitan.net/news/LQDA/liquidia-provides-update-on-hatch-waxman-litigation-tied-to-327-mwttw8wopacb.html)):
  - It will appeal, and it will file a supplement to its NDA to remove PH-ILD from Yutrepia's label.
  - "UTHR has asked the Court for injunctive relief that would restrict YUTREPIA's availability on the market."
  - Remedies "could range from the removal of the indication ... to a broader restriction on product availability". It cannot estimate its exposure.
- UTHR's 10-Q says that if it prevails it expects "a court order requiring Liquidia to remove the PH-ILD indication from Yutrepia's product labeling, and that we may also be entitled to damages."
- Not found as of this evening: a UTHR press release on the ruling, and Liquidia's 8-K on EDGAR.
- There is a precedent for a broad remedy in this same fight:
  - After the 2022 trial on the '793 patent, this court delayed the effective date of Yutrepia's approval to that patent's May 5, 2027 expiry. The Federal Circuit affirmed on Jul 24, 2023 ([FindLaw](https://caselaw.findlaw.com/court/us-federal-circuit/114662918.html)).
  - The '793 claims were later invalidated through a separate Patent Office review (UTHR's 10-Q), which the Federal Circuit upheld on Dec 20, 2023 (footnote 1 of the opinion).
  - In Oct 2025, a legal expert cited by UBS gave about 60% odds that the court would treat the case as Hatch-Waxman. If so, the expert expected Yutrepia's approval date to move to 2042, blocking both PAH and PH-ILD ([Investing.com, Oct 1, 2025](https://www.investing.com/news/analyst-ratings/ubs-maintains-united-therapeutics-stock-buy-rating-amid-patent-litigation-93CH-4266511)).
  - The opinion confirms it is a Hatch-Waxman action. Liquidia will argue that removing PH-ILD from the label is enough.

**How big relative to UTHR.**
- Yutrepia's net sales ([Q2 release](https://www.globenewswire.com/news-release/2026/08/12/3343472/0/en/liquidia-corporation-reports-second-quarter-2026-financial-results.html); [Q2 call](https://www.fool.com/earnings/call-transcripts/2026/08/19/liquidia-lqda-q2-2026-earnings-call-transcript/); [Q1 call](https://www.theglobeandmail.com/investing/markets/stocks/LQDA/pressreleases/1854683/liquidia-lqda-q1-2026-earnings-transcript/)):
  - $90.1M in Q4 2025, $129.9M in Q1 2026 and $170.4M in Q2 2026, about 30% of the inhaled treprostinil market by net revenue.
  - Liquidia guides to more than $1B in 2027.
  - About half of prescriptions are for PH-ILD ("equal scripting between PAH and PH-ILD").
- The PH-ILD half is therefore about $85M a quarter, or about $340M a year at the Q2 run rate.
  - That is 10.8% of UTHR's $3.15B trailing revenue, and 19% of its annualized Q2 Tyvaso revenue.
  - All of Yutrepia, about $680M a year, equals 22% of UTHR's revenue.
- Tyvaso is UTHR's core business ([Q2 release](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1082554/000108255426000028/uthrq22026-ex991.htm)):
  - Q2 revenue was $783.3M (-2%). Tyvaso DPI was $326.6M (+4%) and Nebulized Tyvaso $126.0M (-18%).
  - Total Tyvaso was $452.6M (-4%), 58% of revenue.
  - UTHR blamed "the availability of competitive therapies". It does not disclose its own PH-ILD vs PAH split (not found).
- The market reaction:
  - UTHR rose $60.42 (+12.5%) to $541.89 on 4.4x normal volume, about +$2.6B of market value.
  - LQDA fell 57% to $30.26, about -$3.2B ([Yahoo](https://finance.yahoo.com/healthcare/articles/liquidia-slumps-us-court-rules-194118306.html)).
- What the $60 move already prices. My assumptions: an 80% pre-tax margin on the added sales, a 20% tax rate, 42.9M shares, and UTHR's own 18x forward P/E.
  - If UTHR won back all $340M of PH-ILD sales a year, that is about $5.08 of EPS, or about $92 a share. 80% of it is about $73 a share; 50% is about $46.
  - So the $60 move already prices about two-thirds of today's PH-ILD sales coming back to UTHR.
  - More upside needs a broad remedy, restricting Yutrepia beyond PH-ILD. A label change that leaves off-label PH-ILD use in place would be worth much less.

**Firm vs contingent.**
- Firm: Liquidia's liability on claims 1 and 14 at the trial level, and a patent that runs to Feb 3, 2042. Liquidia's stipulation to indirect (induced) infringement matters for how far an injunction can reach.
- Still to be decided:
  - The remedy. The form of judgment is due about Oct 7, and the judge decides if the parties disagree.
  - Damages for PH-ILD sales since the June 2025 launch.
  - Whether any injunction is put on hold while Liquidia appeals.
  - The Federal Circuit appeal itself. The last round in this fight was decided about a year after the trial ruling.
  - How the FDA handles Liquidia's label change.
  - Whether doctors keep using a PAH-only Yutrepia for PH-ILD off-label.
- Other open cases ([10-Q](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1082554/000108255426000027/uthr-20260630.htm)):
  - UTHR's trade-secret suit against Liquidia and its former executive Dr. Roscigno, in North Carolina state court: trial in January 2027.
  - UTHR's '782 patent suit (the patent expires May 14, 2027): the court denied a preliminary injunction.
  - Liquidia's '494 patent suit against Tyvaso DPI (the patent expires May 5, 2037): on hold until the trade-secret case ends.
  - Boehringer's Jul 17, 2026 suit over UTHR hiring its sales reps.
