## Deep dive (written)

*Written Wed Sep 30, 2026, after the close (the news came at 4:00 PM ET). Not held in either book. The after-hours level of
about $317 (+9.7%) is from the evening scan; it was not re-checked here.*

**What changed.** Vicor raised its Q3 guidance twice in nine days, both times on royalties from one new license, and gave no
dollar figures either time.
- Sep 30, 4:00 PM ET: Q3 sequential growth guidance raised "from more than 20% to more than 30% in view of increased royalties
  from the previously announced first non-exclusive license to Vertical Power Delivery (VPD)". No call, no licensee name, no
  split between royalty and product ([release, Stock Titan copy](https://www.stocktitan.net/news/VICR/vicor-corporation-raises-q3-2026-revenue-itqdkfftslv3.html)).
- Mon Sep 21, 4:00 PM ET: raised "from nearly 10% to more than 20% in view of royalties from a recently announced non-exclusive
  license to VPD". The CEO said four leading OEMs and hyperscalers now hold licenses, and that unlicensed hyperscalers first came
  to Vicor after their systems were banned from import over infringing NBMs. He added that "since the first patent to VPD was
  only recently asserted, it is still possible to secure a license at an early stage of escalation with a low royalty rate"
  ([release, Stock Titan copy](https://www.stocktitan.net/news/VICR/vicor-corporation-raises-q3-2026-revenue-yk2virh0imov.html)).
  The stock rose 19.8% on Sep 22 and another 5.5% on Sep 23.
- The license itself came after the Sep 16 close (dated Sep 17 on Vicor's site). It is non-exclusive and lets "a leading AI OEM"
  buy VPD modules covered by Vicor's patents from unlicensed suppliers. The royalty rate is lower if the OEM also buys Vicor's MCM
  current multipliers, and early adopters get "much lower royalty rates"
  ([Vicor](https://www.vicorpower.com/press-room/2026/vicor-licenses-vpd-to-a-leading-ai-oem)). The OEM is unnamed and no revenue
  commitment was disclosed ([Barchart](https://finance.yahoo.com/technology/ai/articles/vicor-shares-moved-higher-ai-173842919.html)).
  The stock rose 17.7% on Sep 17.
- None of the three releases had been filed as an 8-K at the EDGAR check about 4:30 PM ET Sep 30. The latest 8-Ks are the Sep 10
  bylaw amendment and the Aug 27 buyback.

**How big.** The base is Q2 2026 net revenue of $143.35M: product $112.93M and royalties $30.43M
([Q2 release, 8-K ex. 99.1](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/751978/000119312526309538/d115827dex991.htm)).

| Q3 2026 guide | sequential growth | implied Q3 revenue |
|---|---|---|
| Jul 21 (Q2 call) | "nearly 10%" | about $157M (at most $157.7M) |
| Sep 21 | more than 20% | more than $172.0M |
| Sep 30 | more than 30% | more than $186.4M |

- Today's step adds about $14.3M, 8.3% on the previous floor.
- The two steps together add at least $28.7M: +18% on the July plan, and +69% against Q3 2025's $110.4M. Both are attributed
  only to royalties from the VPD license. Product revenue isn't mentioned, so the July product plan presumably stands.
- The new floor is 7.4% above the Q3 consensus of $173.5M (EPS $1.04), which looks like it reflects only the first raise.
- $28.7M is about the size of the whole Q2 royalty line ($30.4M) and twice Q1's ($15.0M).
- A rough Q3 royalty total, my estimate: $5M from the license signed in Q2 (per the CFO), plus about $15M from older licensees at
  their Q1 run rate, plus at least $28.7M from VPD. That comes to about $49M or more, at least 26% of revenue. The company gave no
  split.
- Royalties carry almost no cost of goods. At a 21% tax rate, $28.7M is about $22.6M of net income, or about $0.47 per diluted
  share (47.7M shares) in Q3. Today's step alone is about $0.24.
- Against the company's size it is small: 0.22% of the $13.32B market value at the close for one quarter, and 0.86% ($115M) if
  it repeats every quarter for a year.
- Plan for the year: "over $600 million" of 2026 revenue (Jul 21 call). With Q3 at $186.4M or more, a flat Q4 gives about $629M.

**Firm vs contingent.** Q3 ended the day of the release, so the Q3 figure is close to an actual (the books aren't closed yet),
and "more than 30%" is a floor. Everything after Q3 is contingent. The VPD license's rate, term, minimums and duration are
undisclosed, and the royalty depends on how many VPD modules one unnamed OEM buys from other suppliers.

**One-offs: the question that decides the value, which the company hasn't answered.**
- Vicor's royalty line has reversed before. Q3 2025 royalties of $21.7M included a catch-up. Q4 2025 royalties then fell 33% to
  $14.5M, "the result of a catch up amount included in the Q3 result"
  ([Q4 2025 release](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/751978/000119312526059563/d240534dex991.htm)).
- New licenses have been front-loaded. The license signed in Q2 2026 pays four $5M quarterly payments in year one and $10M a
  quarter in year two, $60M in all. Yet it put $15M into Q2 revenue "as a result of the termination clauses", then $5M in Q3,
  then $10M a quarter for the next four quarters (CFO James Schmidt,
  [Q2 call](https://www.fool.com/earnings/call-transcripts/2026/07/21/vicor-vicr-q2-2026-earnings-call-transcript/)). Its first
  quarter was three times the next one.
- If the VPD license is booked the same way, or includes royalties on modules bought before it was signed (a back-royalty
  catch-up), part of the Q3 amount will not repeat. Q4 royalties could then fall even with a healthy license. The Sep 30 wording,
  "increased royalties", fits either larger ongoing volumes or a bigger true-up. Which of the two: not found.
- Other one-offs in the earnings base:
  - Q2 2026 EPS of $1.04 includes a $10.9M tax benefit (effective rate -27.9%, from share-based compensation deductions). Taxed
    at 21%, it would be about $0.64.
  - Q4 2025 included a $27.3M tax benefit, and Q2 2025 a $45.0M patent settlement.
  - Trailing EPS of $3.11 (P/E 93) is flattered by about $38M of tax benefits, roughly $0.80 a share
    ([10-Q](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/751978/000119312526322462/vicr-20260630.htm)).

**Competition, customers, regulation.**
- The royalties come from legal leverage. The ITC's final determination on Feb 13, 2025 (337-TA-1370) found the '761 and '481
  patents valid and infringed. It issued a limited exclusion order covering power modules and unlicensed computing systems;
  respondents were Delta Electronics, Cyntec, Foxconn, Ingrasys and Quanta
  ([Susman Godfrey](https://www.susmangodfrey.com/wins/susman-godfrey-secures-itc-ruling-banning-importation-of-power-modules-and-unlicensed-computing-systems-infringing-vicor-patents/)).
- A second case, 337-TA-1484, was filed Jan 12, 2026 and instituted in February. It covers non-isolated SAC converters and names
  10 respondents ([Federal Register](https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/2026/02/17/2026-03032/certain-power-converters-circuit-board-assemblies-and-computing-systems-containing-the-same-notice)).
  The CFO put its final determination in 2027 and said new licenses might not come until then, so the September license came
  early. Where the VPD patent was asserted: not found.
- The license lets the OEM buy "otherwise infringing" modules from Vicor's rivals (Monolithic Power, Delta and others make
  vertical power modules) while Vicor collects a royalty. Vicor gets paid on competitors' parts, but the license also
  legitimizes second sources for its own product, and early adopters pay "much lower" rates.
- Customers are AI OEMs and hyperscalers, none named.
